Accessibility Statement

We want CustodyTap to be usable by everyone who has to record custody, including people who use a keyboard, a screen reader or other assistive technology.

Last updated 14 November 2026.

The standard we aim for

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA across both this website and the CustodyTap application. We treat that as the target for new work and for fixing what we find.

What we do

  • Pages are built from semantic HTML, with a single clear heading order and labelled landmarks.
  • Everything is operable by keyboard, and the focus indicator is always visible.
  • Every form field has a programmatically associated label; the sign-in flow is designed to work with a screen reader.
  • Text aims for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and color is never the only way information is conveyed.
  • We respect your prefers-reduced-motion and prefers-color-scheme settings.
  • Interactive targets are sized for touch, which matters because the main way you use CustodyTap is tapping on a phone.
  • Images carry text alternatives, and decorative graphics are hidden from assistive technology.

Known limitations

CustodyTap is a young service in pilot, and we have not yet completed a formal third-party accessibility audit. There may be places that fall short of the target above, and the core task depends on an NFC-capable phone, which is itself a constraint for some users. If you rely on assistive technology and hit a barrier, telling us is the fastest way to get it fixed — see below.

Tell us about a barrier

If any part of CustodyTap is hard to use with assistive technology, email dbrown@enthropicdata.com with the page or screen and what went wrong. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within five business days and will work with you on a way to get what you need in the meantime.
